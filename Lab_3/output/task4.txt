### TASK 4 — OBJECTIVE EVALUATION

Two objective metrics were used to evaluate the performance of different image-processing techniques.

1. Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR):

PSNR was calculated by comparing each processed image with the original clean ground-truth image. A higher PSNR indicates lower reconstruction error.

2. Sharpness (Variance of Laplacian):

The variance of the Laplacian was used to measure high-frequency details and edge strength. A higher value indicates stronger intensity variations but can also result from noise and sharpening artefacts.

Observations:

1. In Task 1, the 3×3 averaging filter achieved the highest PSNR for both noise levels among the tested configurations. Increasing the kernel size reduced sharpness and also reduced PSNR in these experiments.
2. In Task 2, Laplacian sharpening increased the sharpness metric significantly. The 8-neighbour kernel produced a higher sharpness value than the 4-neighbour kernel, but its PSNR was lower.
3. In Task 3, increasing the high-boost parameter k increased the sharpness value. However, PSNR decreased as k increased, indicating greater deviation from the original clean image.

Conclusion:

PSNR and variance of Laplacian provide complementary information about image quality. A higher sharpness value does not necessarily indicate better image quality. Therefore, both metrics should be considered when selecting an appropriate image-processing configuration.
